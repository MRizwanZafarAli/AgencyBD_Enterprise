# Growth playbook: every way to find a client, including the long shots

Written for a small software and web agency starting from zero (Future Builders, based in Pakistan, selling to anywhere). Everything here is free to run.
"Chance" is a rough guide for one honest, well-aimed attempt, not a promise. Treat 1 in 100 as normal, and keep the long shots running in the background.
The app column says what already exists and what is planned. Rules that never change: no scraping of logged-in accounts, no fake identities, no claims you cannot prove, one honest message per person, stop when they say no.

## B. Marketplaces, done properly

1. One narrow, specific offer beats "web developer": "Upgrade your PHP site safely", "Shopify speed fix", "WordPress security clean-up", "Accessibility fixes for small clinics".
2. Fixed-price starter product: a 48-hour website health check for a flat price. Easy to say yes to, and it opens bigger work.
3. Answer inside the first hour. Response speed is what Fiverr and Upwork reward. This is what the Alert Desk is for.
4. Apply to fewer, better-matched jobs. Track reply rate and cost per win (Channels shows both).
5. Ask every delivered client for a review, once. Reviews are the compounding asset.
6. LinkedIn: switch on the Services page, post one useful finding a week, comment on target companies' posts. Use the search-link builder; never automate.

## D. Partnerships: the biggest multiplier

One partner can send ten clients. Offer white-label development and a referral fee to:
design and branding studios, marketing and SEO agencies, freelance designers and photographers, accountants and company-formation agents (they meet new businesses first), lawyers, IT support firms, hosting and domain resellers, coworking spaces, incubators and accelerators, and other developers who are fully booked.
In Pakistan, look at Plan9, NIC, P@SHA members and PSEB-registered companies, and Pakistani businesses in Dubai, Saudi Arabia and the UK who want a trusted team back home.

## E. Local and offline (strong in Pakistan)

Many businesses run on Facebook or WhatsApp Business only. Walk in or phone: clinics, schools and academies, restaurants, real-estate agents, garment and leather exporters, Daraz and Instagram sellers. Pitch "your own website and Google listing, so customers find you without paying Facebook". Bring a printed one-page health report of their current site or page.

## F. Signals the app can watch for you (Lead Finder does this for new UK companies, jobs, and problems on sites you checked)

Slow site, no HTTPS, outdated PHP or WordPress, no mobile layout, no booking system, "coming soon" pages, a competitor with a better site, a new Product Hunt, BetaList or Kickstarter launch needing polish, apps with poor ratings, newly expired domains, a new map listing, a grand opening in the news, a company that just raised money, a change of owner, public complaints about a website.

## G. Long shots (0.1% to 1%, cheap to keep running)

Cold LinkedIn connection with a specific note; short cold email sequences; a phone call to a published business number; speaking at or sponsoring a local meetup or hackathon; guest posts; the monthly "Who wants to be hired" threads on Hacker News and r/forhire; UN, NGO and World Bank tenders (ReliefWeb, UNGM); reviving old classmates, colleagues and past clients ("here is exactly what I do now"); a referral program (10% of the first project); awards and directory listings; answering Trustpilot and Google reviews that complain about a company's website; university alumni networks; cold DMs to founders after genuinely helping them with something.

## Rules that keep you safe and credible

- Unsolicited WhatsApp or SMS to strangers is the fastest way to lose a number. Use email, a contact form or a phone call, and WhatsApp only after they reply or ask.
- In the EU, UK and Ireland, business email is allowed when relevant and honest, with a clear way to opt out. Keep one message, one claim, one offer.
- Quote only what you can reproduce. If you cannot repeat a measurement, do not use it.
- Never imitate a person or agency. Never state a result you cannot show.
